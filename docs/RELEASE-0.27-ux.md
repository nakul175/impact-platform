# v0.27 — Console dialogs, human labels, access gate and refreshed guides (UX slice S2)

Branch `ux/0.27-console-and-guides` from `main` at 25a8dca (build 0.26.0, schema 28, domain API 1.16.0, platform API 1.7.0). No version, schema, contract or policy change; the integrator bumps the build. Ledger: 307 requirements, 106 PARTIAL, 201 PENDING, 0 accepted — this slice moves nothing (FR-UX-004 and VF-UX-001 gain browser evidence only).

## Why

A training-video recording with the product owner (a non-engineer) exposed seven defects of presentation, none of them of authorisation: the console reopened for the next person after sign-out, the console's change forms opened out of sight at the foot of a long page, the period-close dialog spoke in codes and identifier prefixes, the review-queue record showed raw JSON, an owner awaiting initial access was greeted with "The action is not permitted" and a sidebar of areas they could not use, and the two guides still said that exports were not implemented. This increment fixes them in the web client, adds one read-only field to `GET me/access`, extends three browser groups and rewrites both guides for build 0.26.0.

## Delivered

**1. Sign-out resets every per-person state** (`apps/web/src/main.tsx`). `logout()` and the forced re-authentication path share one `forget()` that clears the CSRF token, the session, the selected tenant and the `tenantConsole` flag. The next person to sign in on the same tab starts at their own landing page. An invitation read from the URL is kept: it belongs to the link, not the person (a wrong-account sign-out on the join page still returns to the join page).

**2. Console change forms open as a dialog next to the card** (`apps/web/src/TenantLifecycle.tsx`). The tenant request, accept-ownership, activate, suspend, reactivate and begin-closure forms render inside the existing `Dialog` component (passed from `main.tsx` as a prop, as every workspace panel receives it): modal, titled with the human action name and the organisation ("Activate tenant: Lifecycle browser …"), the refusal shown inside it, Escape and × cancel, and focus returns to the card button that opened it (`Dialog`'s own focus-return). `aria-label="Tenant change"` is gone; the dialog is found by role. Initial access, recovery contacts and authority renewal keep their foot-of-page forms (shorter pages; not changed in this slice).

**3. Human labels.** Period close (`apps/web/src/PeriodGovernance.tsx`): programmes, periods and sources show "Title (CODE)" instead of the code; the review policy shows its title (reference-data templates carry one) and the fixture's untitled template shows what it is, "Independent review, 1 approval (ff396e2e)", never a bare prefix. Review queue (`main.tsx` `ReviewStages`): a workflow record's `stages` render as a list — stage number and kind, who may decide (named reviewers or any holder of the capability, never an author of the submitted version), approvals required, and the decision with its time ("Awaiting a decision · submitted 2 Oct 2026, 13:47" or "Approved · …"); the remaining fields render as before, and the raw stage objects stay in the record.

**4. Access gate instead of a permission error** (`apps/web/src/AccessGate.tsx`, `main.tsx`). The workspace now has an explicit access state (`loading` / `ready` / `failed`). While `GET me/access` is unanswered the sidebar and the page heading render as they always did — `workspace-browser` deliberately holds that answer back to prove the settings panel initialises after late permissions, so nothing may be gated before it — but the record list is not requested until the answer is known, so the 403 that produced "The action is not permitted" is never sent. When the answer holds no capability and no purpose-bound capability, the content is a waiting page: for the owner of the custody membership, "Your administrator access is being set up" with the three outstanding steps (owner proposes, second administrator accepts, an operator who is neither approves) and buttons **Check again** and **Open the Tenant lifecycle console**; for any other grant-less member, "Your access is being set up" and whom to ask. Once the answer is known the sidebar lists an area only when the person holds at least one capability it can use (`areaCapabilities`, a prefix map per area; My account always; purpose-bound capabilities count for People & access). The landing route is the portfolio, else People & access, else the first listed area. Hidden entries are presentation only: the server authorises every request as before.

The one API change: `Service.access` (`apps/api/impact_api/service.py`) adds `custody: true|false` — whether the caller's membership is the tenant's custody membership (one `tenant_custody` SELECT the app role already holds). It is informational, like `purpose_capabilities` added in v0.25b; the Access response schema was not changed (neither was it for `purpose_capabilities`).

**5. Start page copy** (`apps/web/src/Landing.tsx`). "No active workspace" now tells a nominated operator what an operator does and that only they can accept, and a nominated owner what accepting means and that administrator access follows the initial-access review; the operator's five steps and the generic paragraph are unchanged. The heading, the banners and the identity reference that `operators-browser` asserts are unchanged.

**6. Guides** (`docs/current/USER-GUIDE.md`, `docs/current/ADMINISTRATOR-GUIDE.md`): rewritten for build 0.26.0, structured by task (sign in; find your way; set up a programme; results framework; enter data by hand, form or spreadsheet; evidence; review; results; close a period; dashboards; reports, exports and publication; corrections; errors; exclusions — and for administrators: onboarding steps, the operators' console, authority renewal, invitations and grants, roles, reference data, audit export, data-subject requests, suspension and closure, incidents). Each section names its release note; every control named was checked against the client source; limits are stated where the release notes state them. No screenshots were added.

**7. Browser checks.** Three pre-existing probes had to follow the new presentation: `check.mjs` matched the review record by lower-case `candidate` text that only the raw JSON supplied (now a case-insensitive probe that also waits for the submitted revision to load), its narrow-viewport step clicked "People & access" as the reviewer, who holds no such capability and no longer sees the entry (now "Review queue"), and `tenant-check.mjs`'s short-screen test counted the administrator's entries, which are now few (now the author's). `tenant-browser` (`tools/browser/tenant-check.mjs`): every change goes through the dialog (`confirm` scopes to it; `change` asserts the dialog heading "Action: organisation", that focus is inside it and that it is detached after the save); the self-activation refusal is asserted inside the dialog, Cancel detaches it and focus is back on "Activate tenant"; new "Owner awaiting initial access sees what happens next, not a permission error" (the owner's `me/access` is custody with no capability; the workspace shows the waiting page, no alert, two sidebar entries; switching back to the fixture workspace lists ≥10 again); new "Signing out of the console starts the next person in their own workspace" (sign out from the console, sign in as reviewer, land on the portfolio with no console heading). `browser` (`check.mjs`): the reviewer's record dialog shows "Review stages" with "Stage 1 · independent review", "Who may decide" and "Awaiting a decision · submitted", and no `"stage_id"`. `reporting-browser` (`reporting-check.mjs`): the period-close dialog's programme options match `Title (CODE)` and no review-policy option is a bare eight-hex prefix.

## Contract and persistence

None. No migration, no contract regeneration, no policy row. `GET /v1/tenants/{t}/me/access` gains the field `custody` (boolean); `openapi.json`'s design-level `Access` schema is unchanged, as it was for `purpose_capabilities`.

## Limits

- For the duration of the `me/access` request (normally well under a second) the full sidebar is listed; it narrows when the answer arrives. Gating it earlier would break the late-permissions scenario `workspace-browser` proves.
- The access gate decides on capabilities alone. A member whose grants are all scoped to objects they cannot read still sees the areas; a member with a stale `me/access` presses **Check again** (no polling).
- The area-to-capability map is hand-written (`areaCapabilities` in `main.tsx`); a new area or capability family must be added there or its entry stays hidden for people who hold only the new capability.
- Initial access, recovery contacts and authority renewal pages still open their forms at the foot of the page.
- "Who may decide" names reviewers by count and capability, not by display name (the queue record carries membership identifiers only, and a reviewer may lack `memberships.read`).
- The decision time shown for a decided review is the workflow record's `updated_at`; the decision record itself (route `decisions`) is not fetched.
- `custody` is informational and must never be used to authorise anything on the client; the server does not read it.
- The guides have no screenshots; `docs/images/` was not created.
